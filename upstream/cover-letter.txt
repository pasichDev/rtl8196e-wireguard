Subject: [PATCH net-next 0/1] wireguard: skip the crypt workqueue on UP

WireGuard parallelises crypto over CPUs with packet_crypt_wq and restores
order through per-peer queues. On a uniprocessor kernel the workqueue cannot
add parallelism, but every packet still pays for the hand-offs: two
workqueue hops on transmit, one on receive, each with a context switch.

Single-core MIPS routers are a common OpenWrt target. On one of them (Realtek
RTL8196E, Lexra RLX4181 at about 400 MHz) WireGuard ran at about 9 Mbit/s
while plain TCP to the same board ran at 73-88 Mbit/s. Throughput was nearly
independent of packet size, and the kernel PC profiler put about 22% of CPU
time on context switches, with about 0.85 per tunnelled packet.

The patch keeps the per-peer queues and their reference handling, and on UP
encrypts inline before draining the transmit queue, and decrypts in the
peer's NAPI poll. On that board receive went from 8.9 to 19.6 Mbit/s and send
from 7.9 to 13.8 Mbit/s; details are in the commit message. SMP builds
compile the new branches out.

Open questions for review:

- Is a !CONFIG_SMP special case acceptable here, or would you prefer a
  runtime condition (e.g. num_possible_cpus() == 1) so that SMP kernels on
  single-CPU machines benefit too?
- On transmit the whole GSO batch is encrypted with bottom halves disabled.
  On this CPU a 64 KB batch takes tens of milliseconds. No packet loss was
  seen, but a budget (or deferring to the existing worker past one) may be
  preferable.

Tested only on the board above with Linux 6.18.54; I would be glad of results
from other uniprocessor devices.
